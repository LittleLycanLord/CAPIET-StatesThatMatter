--- Section: Background of the Study ---

The foundational knowledge of chemistry is important for students to train through early on in their academic careers in order to better understand more complex concepts in the future, as well as learn their real-world applications.

In regards to this, the main factor as to why students could get overwhelmed and disinterested is due to how current learning materials in the academic field were not structured to ease or motivate. What tends to happen inside of a classroom is that students are expected to immediately apply the multitude of knowledge handed to them with expertise rather than given a proper time and space to practice it. Students tend to have difficulty in learning chemistry due to this lack of practicality and interactivity with the material. A more hands-on approach in mastering these fundamentals would help them better form these difficult concepts into their respective abstractions. Interactive learning allows students to actively participate in educational processes, and hone their critical thinking and analytical skills to apply these hands-on experiences in the real world. With instant feedback as well, it allows them to better understand how to correct the misconceptions they may have with their learning materials as soon as possible.

With this in mind, these are the factors that contribute immensely to their lack of motivation to study and practice this branch of chemistry. This especially is a concern given that before heading into changes in matter as a topic, they must master prerequisites in chemistry beforehand in order to fully understand the intricacies of this topic. Instead, these materials end up becoming too difficult that it demotivates learners from using it to their full advantage, as well as they are not typically given the option to fully absorb and understand new concepts before heading onto the next as imposed by the academic curriculum.

Given these concerns, an approach that would encourage students to actively engage with learning materials without overloading themselves with tasks, whilst simultaneously improving their critical thinking and cognitive skills, is one that would be most beneficial to allow students to gain a better learning experience when it comes to the changes in matter. Accessibility and relevance to its intended audience may also prove to improve comprehension of the topics at hand. This study aims to build an approach that is also relevant to the curriculum of the students in order to shape a better flow in their learning process, acting as a tool for students to combat their disinterest in the materials and channeling it into engagement and motivation. This not only improves on their practical skills, but also aims to improve the emotional impact on the students from what would otherwise be a continued cycle of discouragement that often arises with traditional learning methods.

Furthermore, a Game-Based Learning (GBL) approach that would be developed as a supplemental material to the curriculum of students learning the foundations of chemistry will strengthen their cognitive and critical thinking skills. This, in turn, will help build their understanding of this difficult branch of chemistry in the long run.

--- Section: Literature Review on the Educational Challenges in the Philippines in both a General and Scientific Scope ---

The K-12 program, which is the currently implemented educational program in the Philippines, has presented multiple challenges with the main one being curriculum congestion. There is an overload in subject topics and projects for each semester which brings intense pressure for both students and teachers. Teachers struggle to fulfill the curriculum standards in terms of covering all the designated topics in the set duration of time. Other challenges faced are the lack of resources and the shortage of skilled teachers. With the flaws of the K-12 program made known, the Department of Education (DepEd) has introduced a new curriculum called the MATATAG Curriculum. The MATATAG Curriculum aims to solve the current challenges and to truly respond to the students needs. It prioritizes the cultivation of critical thinking, digital literacy, and global competence among Filipino students. However, at this current time, the MATATAG curriculum is still in development and needs continuous evaluation.

Most schools in the Philippines are currently following a hybrid learning approach even though COVID-19 pandemic has already passed. With the introduction of online classes, we wanted to see how much Filipino students have adapted to it and if they encounter any issues. According to research results, the issues on the online setup vary from one student to another. It mostly depends on the students' resources, connection to their teachers and peers, and socioeconomic status. It has been discovered that the status of the learning environment at home is the source of most issues while technological literacy and competency is the source of the least issues.

There is a need for curriculum reform for Science subjects due to multiple issues like mismatching of prerequisite skills and knowledge and poor performance of students in both national and international assessments. The Science Curriculum under MATATAG aims to improve technology and engineering literacy of Filipino students and align them to the demands of the 21st century, Industry 4.0, and Industry 5.0. For the situation of chemistry learning in terms of online learning and teaching approaches, it has been revealed that instructional approaches enhance students' academic performances and participation. And included in instructional approaches are computer simulations and project based e-learning. Other fitting learning approaches for Chemistry are inquiry-based and cooperative learning. Inquiry-based learning is a mode where the students are driven by their own curiosity and try to explore the topics themselves. Cooperative learning is when students work together with their peers in projects.

--- Section: Literature Review on Educational Serious Games ---

In the papers the difference between a serious game and gamification has been clarified. Gamification is the use of game design elements in non-game contexts while serious games are digital games that have a characterizing goal and an entertainment goal. And a study showed that gamification and serious games do impact learning achievement differently, with gamification having a stronger positive impact in both achievement and motivation. Gamification has a stronger effect on extrinsic motivation while serious games have a stronger effect on intrinsic motivation. When it comes to serious games an outcome-based methodology for development is advised. It is a methodology wherein learning outcomes are the determining factors for most of the game's elements.

--- Section: Literature Review on Chemistry game-based learning and existing Chemistry Games ---

GBL or Game-Based learning encourages active learning both hands-on and minds-on. It leads to a deeper comprehension of the topic. It is effective because it implements a practical, fun, and interesting way of learning. The use of digital games can improve critical thinking skills, learning retention, and even motivation.

Existing educational chemistry games are mostly puzzle games like Sokobond Express and Element Solitaire (Elementeo). But there are also chemistry games that are RPGs and simulation type ones.

Stoichiometry is the most difficult topic under chemistry and most students struggle in learning it due to complicated traditional learning materials, lack of motivation, and boring discussions. Which is why delving into serious games as learning material because it has the potential to motivate and inspire students. There is a study conducted that used a snakes and ladders type game to possibly improve student's motivation. It touched upon topics like molecular mass calculation and empirical formulas for compounds. Even though the board game is not a serious game due to its non-digital nature, it has shown to have improved the chemistry learning of the students.

--- Section: Literature Review on Bubble Shooter Games for Learning ---

Bubble shooter type games help with memorization and visualization due to its repetitive nature. The bubble-shooter games that are featured in the papers like Bias Blaster and Chemistry Pop are not published games and are mostly made just for a research study.

--- Section: Game Overview ---

[GAME NAME HERE] is a 2D bubbleshooter edugame that teaches Grade 6 chemistry by letting students create different materials through five action orbs. Each orb causes a change which can either be physical or chemical. Other than the bubbleshooter levels, the players can also explore and interact with the game's village and aid neighbors through a sorting minigame. --- Section: Game-Concept ---

Local Bubble Chemist is a 2D stylized educational game that is designed for Grade 6 learners, the game is set in a brightly colored neighborhood and follows a chemist along on her daily tasks to help around. The tasks given to her are mainly focused on core chemistry topics such as states of matter, and more importantly, differentiating between physical and chemical change in our daily lives. Upon facing a challenge, specifically in the game's beginning levels set in the cafe, players are to shoot action orbs onto ingredients to see how their properties change and to encourage them to reach certain goals through a recipe system. It is also made known to players that they are making progress through a traffic light coloring system in the orbs, wherein red means they have made no progress, yellow means they have started making progress, and green indicates completion of a recipe. Players must utilize the orbs, namely cooling, heating, mixing, slicing, crushing and swapping, as a means to achieve the correct outcomes as needed per level.

--- Section: Genre ---

It spans multiple genres, combining educational gaming (EduGame) with puzzle and simulation elements. The bubble-shooter gameplay positions it firmly within the puzzle genre, requiring timing, logic, and accuracy to solve chemistry-based challenges. Meanwhile the neighborhood functions as a simulation/exploration space, encouraging curiosity and reinforcing knowledge through interactive storytelling. This hybrid genre approach supports both active recall and contextual understanding, making it an effective learning tool.

The game progresses by solving the different problems presented in the first location which is the cafe, and after each level a player completes they would be able to move onto harder levels which consists of more ingredients and more action orbs that results in new combinations and reactions. --- Section: Gameplay and Mechanics - Gameplay ---

The core gameplay objective takes place entirely inside the cafe, where the Local Bubble Chemist presents players with science-based challenges focused on fundamental chemistry concepts such as changes in states of matter, distinguishing between physical and chemical changes, and understanding how heat and temperature affect materials. Each challenge begins with a bubble-shooter sequence in which players launch action based orbs like Heat, Cool, Crush, and Cut toward target zones to trigger the correct reactions. Players must aim carefully, as incorrect or wasted shots can lead to penalties with instant visual and auditory feedback showing the results of each action in real time. As players progress, the challenges grow more complex, introducing multi-step reactions and environmental modifiers, with the Bubble Chemist providing guidance and hints to ensure players understand the scientific reasoning behind each solution.

--- Section: Gameplay and Mechanics - Mechanics ---

Set in a narrative-driven world, players must take on tasks to help around their local community and handle tasks revolving around physical and chemical change. These challenges are ones that can be seen in everyday life, such as in cooking ingredients or identifying which ones have gone bad, and this is emulated in the game as a means to showcase how these properties can change all around us at all times. Each time a challenge arises, the player is to use their designated bubble shooter with action orbs such as cooling, heating, crushing, and swapping onto a path of items to see how these actions will affect them. This simulates scientific exploration in the context of everyday life through an accessible arcade style format. Players must also apply their knowledge in such a way that simple science principles can be used to solve each problem. Incorrect use of orbs, ineffective combinations, or failure to activate the right solutions may result in penalties for the player. The traffic light system of the orbs on the path will show the player their progress, as well as a 3-star scoring system allowing the players to easily visualize their performance throughout the level.

--- Section: Gameplay and Mechanics - Physics ---

The game uses simple bubble shooter mechanics to simulate the bouncing and collision of action orbs within the experimental area. Each orb moves with predictable trajectories and interacts with other orbs on basic collision rules, helping players develop timing and aim while keeping gameplay intuitive and consistent.

The movement also applies with the exploration wherein the joystick can be moved to walk in specific directions.

There is no traditional combat in [GAME NAME HERE]. Instead the challenge involves solving chemistry puzzles and overcoming obstacles such as limited resources, timing constraints, or conditions that mimic reaction requirements.

--- Section: Gameplay and Mechanics - Economy ---

To complete a bubbleshooter level, the player must achieve creating a specific number of products per level. For example in the tutorial level of the game which occurs in a cafe, the player is tasked to complete fifteen iced coffees and fifteen hot coffees to progress. The bubbleshooter genre on its own requires players to precisely shoot the balls onto the track in a correct order to achieve the desired product, so it's prioritized that the vision of the player is not too obstructed with irrelevant user-interface elements, while still retaining that key information is still obvious at a moment's glance, such as the illustration in the material and action orbs.

--- Section: Interface - Control System ---

Given the nature of our game, we are implementing a screen-based or screen-focused type of controls, where most of the players' interactions are via clicking and/or dragging via the mouse or via touchscreen, so as to easily shift from PC to mobile platforms, depending on which is more available to our sample playerbase.

--- Section: Interface - Audio, music, sound effects ---

We are using a mix of both in-house, open-source, and third-party audio sourced online, which will have proper crediting and licensing. This applies, but is not limited, to sound effects, voices, and background music or audio, either created on our own via music composition software or in-house recordings, or sourced online from websites that provide audio samples, libraries, and assets.

--- Section: Interface - Help System ---

The help system in the game is designed to directly support learners throughout gameplay. This would be accessible both in the main menu and pause screen, it provides simple tutorials, definitions of key chemistry terms, gameplay instructions, and visual examples of compound formation. This system allows students to review concepts at their own pace, reinforcing understanding without disrupting immersion. It functions as a built-in learning companion, ensuring that educational content remains accessible and clearly explained at all times.

--- Section: Formative User Testing Observations ---

Formative user testing was conducted with Grade 6 students from Inchican Elementary School, using an unfinished but playable prototype that included the Tutorial Level, Bubble Shooter Level 1, and the Sorting Mini Game. Testing was carried out face-to-face, allowing direct observation of player-behavior, engagement, and comprehension. Feedback highlighted several key areas for improvement. Students expressed that the levels felt too easy and were quickly memorized, suggesting the need for additional challenge variety. Some commented that the food items in the sorting mini game would look better, reinforcing visual consistency across gameplay sections. They also suggested adding more objects to sort to make the activity feel less quick and more engaging. Language accessibility was another major theme, with students requesting Filipino/Tagalog text to make instructions easier to understand. In general, players enjoyed the experience "Masaya naman po" but noted they would prefer to play the completed version of the game. This feedback will guide refinements to difficulty balance, visual design, and language options in future iterations.

--- Section: Research Design ---

We will be performing a mixed method approach for data collection. For the qualitative approach, one-on-one interviews will be conducted both during and after the creation of the game. The one-on-one interviews during the game development will provide us information on what Grade 6 students are interested in by knowing their favorite games to play. We will use their favorite games as reference so we can create an educational game that caters to them. On the other hand, the interviews conducted after the students played [GAME NAME HERE], will reveal how enjoyable the experience is. Then for the quantitative approach, the group will conduct pretests and posttests for a whole month to gauge the effectiveness of [GAME NAME HERE] as an educational game. The test results will reveal how much the students' knowledge on the changing states of matter increased through the bubble-shooter and simulation gameplay.

--- Section: Data Collection ---

The primary data will be collected from the interview inputs and pretests and posttests results. Both will be conducted to 20 Grade 6 students and through a face-to-face setting. Each member of the group will be assigned 5 students to interview privately which will be voice recorded for an easy data analysis procedure. The division of students also applies for the live face-to-face playtesting that will be conducted in relation to the pretests and posttests.

The procedure with the quantitative side will begin with the pretests that will be done in person. Before the students see and play the game, their initial knowledge of the properties of matter will be tested through a 25 item quiz. The students will be given approximately 40 minutes to answer the entire test. After the pretests, the students will be asked to play the game in a span of a month. To make sure that they indeed play the game, members of Novaluno Studios will monitor them weekly as they play live. We are not allowed to converse or give hints about the game. After every live playtesting, the Grade 6 students will immediately answer a posttest that will also be 25-items total and can be answered in a span of 40 minutes.

Likewise, the interviews will be done in a face-to-face setup with one Grade 6 student being assigned to one Novaluno Studios member. Guide questions will be prepared beforehand so that the group can collect all the necessary information in a structured manner. The interviews will be recorded through a phone recording so that it can be transcribed for data analysis. Since the interviews will be conducted during and after [GAME NAME HERE]'s development, a total of 40 interviews will be performed.

Our secondary data will comprise a range of player and gameplay analytics collected directly within the game. These analytics will be designed and implemented in-house by the development team to ensure greater control, customization, and compatibility with low-end devices. All participants will be informed of the nature and purpose of these data prior to inclusion in the target sample. The analytics will initially be stored offline and will be periodically transmitted online once a stable internet connection is available.

While the specific variables for collection have yet to be finalized, examples may include the time spent by a player on specific levels, screens, features, or dialogues; the rate of progression through the game; points at which players reach specific milestones or stages; and locations or challenges where players become stuck. Once gathered, these analytics will provide supplementary data that can be correlated with other findings and incorporated into the study's conclusions.

The implementation of these analytics will also serve as a framework for the proper integration of data tracking systems in future educational games. By identifying the most relevant metrics, optimizing their collection for varying device capabilities, and ensuring ethical transparency with participants, this approach can inform best practices for balancing educational goals with player engagement. Such a framework can guide developers in designing analytics that not only measure performance but also yield actionable insights for improving gameplay, instructional design, and learner outcomes in subsequent edugame projects.

--- Section: Data Analysis ---

Repeated measures ANOVA or analysis of variance will be used to analyze the difference between the pretests and posttests scores. The repeated measures ANOVA is an extension of the t-test for dependent samples wherein three or more dependent samples are examined. This is the most fitting tool to analyze the test results since we will be measuring the same student's improvement at different points in time.

When it comes to the data from the interviews, the voice recordings will first be transcribed. Once all the inputs are written, it will be analyzed through thematic analysis. Thematic analysis is used to extract and analyze themes from a diverse range of data and is mostly used in interviews and focus group discussions. It has the ability to uncover rich and nuanced conclusions and explore different perspectives. The first step in thematic analysis is the familiarization with the data, the written transcripts from the focused group discussions will be thoroughly read with all the valuable concepts highlighted. The next steps are generating codes and searching for themes, codes will be implemented to organize the overwhelming data and will then be grouped into specific themes to best represent the data inputs. Writing the thematic analysis and interpreting the findings are the last steps and it serves as the final synthesis of the results.

--- Section: Ethical Considerations ---

All participants will be sent an informed consent form that they need to fill out and sign. The consent form will contain the goals and purpose of [GAME NAME HERE], basis of participation, data collection procedures, and confidentiality. Since our target participants are minors, we will be sending a parental consent form addressed to their parents. We will make sure that all the students that will participate in our project will remain anonymous and that they will also have the freedom to withdraw at any time in the instance that they experience discomfort. Once our research is finished, all data that includes our participants will be disposed of.

Potential risks include the game covering concepts that are not covered in the classes of the participants, or are covered in a different way. This may introduce misconceptions that could affect the academic performance of the participant. This is going to be mitigated by working closely with teachers and educational content experts, aligning the content of the game with classroom discussions. Several rounds of intermediate playtesting would also be conducted with the target demographic to gauge how they would react to the game and what the game covers. Adjustments would be made should conflicts with classroom instruction be detected, further reducing the risk of introducing misconceptions.
