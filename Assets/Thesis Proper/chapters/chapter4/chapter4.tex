\section{Software Overview}

\textit{Wiz's Journey: Mastering the States of Matter} is a mobile-based Game-Based Learning Environment (GBLE) designed to reinforce the targeted \chg{learning outcome} from the MATATAG Grade 6 Science curriculum: ``describe changes of state for solids, liquids, and gases as melting, evaporation, freezing, and condensation using diagrams and flowcharts.'' The design leverages intrinsic motivation through gameplay progression mechanics rather than relying exclusively on external incentive structures. Players advance through the game by successfully applying conceptual knowledge of phase transitions to overcome in-game challenges, creating a direct link between pedagogical mastery and game-based progression.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\chg{Platformer}}{Platformer}}

The \chg{platformer} mechanics encompass the platformer-based navigation subsystem. Following the Outcomes-Based Edugame Design framework (Sison et al., 2018), the platformer serves as the narrative and mechanical premise, establishing a coherent game world that requires spatial navigation from an entry point to an exit objective. This design pattern is grounded in established game design principles exemplified by seminal titles such as \textit{Super Mario Bros}, wherein level progression follows a directional narrative structure---typically left-to-right progression culminating in a goal object or adversary encounter.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\chg{Particle Lattice Minigame}}{Particle Lattice Minigame}}

The \chg{Particle Lattice Minigame} mechanics are operationalized through the Particle Lattice System---an internally developed subsystem that generates particle configuration diagrams representing the microscopic structural arrangement of solids, liquids, and gases. This system was engineered with extensibility as a primary design principle to accommodate multiple phases, materials, and parameter presets configured by developers. This architecture enables potential implementations of diverse materials beyond water---the conventional exemplar in phase-change pedagogy---such as steel or clay. However, implementation scope was constrained by development timeline limitations.

The system implements parametric configurability through developer-accessible parameters that can be set and persisted for material simulation. Key configurable properties include particle attributes (size, initial temperature, quantity, spatial arrangement) and intermolecular bond characteristics (length, stiffness, elasticity). This parametric foundation provides pedagogical extensibility while maintaining implementational efficiency.

\centering

Within \textit{Wiz's Journey: Mastering the States of Matter}, the Particle Lattice System generates interactive minigame instances that players encounter repeatedly throughout level progression. When players encounter environmental obstacles that impede forward progress---such as pit hazards, ice blocks, or water barriers---an interaction affordance (visual button prompt) appears on-screen, enabling players to engage the minigame interface associated with that obstacle.

Player interaction with material objects reveals particle lattice configurations. Players manipulate particles through direct interaction (dragging), experiencing firsthand the elastic, rigid, or compliant properties of different matter states through haptic feedback and visual responsivity. Lattice visualizations are designed to correspond with canonical pedagogical diagrams found in standard science curricula, ensuring cognitive alignment with existing mental models from classroom instruction. \subsection{\texorpdfstring{\chg{Synthesis of Platformer and Particle Lattice Minigame}}{Synthesis of Platformer and Particle Lattice Minigame}}

Challenge scenarios are introduced through explicit narrative dialogue that specifies the required phase transition but withholds procedural methodology. Players are left to independently discover the application mechanics of specified phase transitions, fostering autonomous problem-solving and reinforcing the causal relationship between \chg{minigame interactions} and \chg{platformer} environmental state changes.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{ExplicitInstruction.png}
\caption{Player being told to MELT the ice}
\label{fig:explicit_instruction}
\end{figure}

This pedagogical coupling of \chg{platformer} and \chg{minigame} systems positions players in continuous active experimentation cycles. The game environment facilitates iterative hypothesis testing regarding phase-transition mechanisms and effects. This cyclical gameplay pattern---characteristic of game-loop design (Cook, 2006)---enables players to internalize conceptual models through repeated experimentation and observation. The expectation is that through sustained gameplay iteration, players will develop robust mental models of matter state structures, thermodynamic mechanisms, and phase-transition flowcharts that transfer to real-world contexts.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.75\textwidth]{DanielCook.png}
\caption{Daniel Cook's Game Loop Diagram}
\label{fig:game_loop}
\end{figure}

User interface evolution involved iterative simplification to minimize cognitive load. Early prototypes employed color-coded particles, colored bond visualizations, dynamic object rotation, and extensive on-screen text---design choices that generated player confusion and decision paralysis. The final interface implementation prioritizes essential information through: (1) unadorned particle lattice with kinetic energy representation (particle oscillation intensity correlates with thermal energy; bond rigidity visually decreases with entropy), (2) spellbook sprite icons replacing descriptive text labels, (3) explicit phase-transition labels (e.g., ``Melting: Solid to Liquid''), and (4) confirmation/cancellation affordances enabling committed action.

\section{Game Aesthetics}

Visual aesthetic design prioritizes accessibility and clarity appropriate to the target demographic (Grade 6 students, aged 11--12 years). The interface employs minimalist design principles, restricting interactive affordances to necessary control elements to minimize decision complexity. The snowy mountain environment and magical narrative premise provide thematic coherence for utilizing phase-transition materials (ice, water, vapor) throughout level design. Visual design incorporates bright, saturated colors and stylistically appealing elements to maintain affective engagement.

Technical implementation leverages 2.5-dimensional visual presentation through parallax scrolling and Unity 6's Cinemachine camera system, achieving a distinctive aesthetic combining retro pixel-art styling with contemporary smooth animation. This approach provides visual novelty while maintaining accessibility through clear visual communication.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{PopUpBookAesthetic.png}
\caption{Popup Book Appearance}
\label{fig:aesthetic_popup}
\end{figure}

Visual and audio asset acquisition prioritized pragmatic efficiency. Assets were sourced from open-source repositories (Itch.io, Open Game Art) and premium asset markets (Unity Asset Store), with modifications applied where permitted by licensing agreements to ensure originality. This production approach was necessitated by limited development personnel. Asset categories include sprites, textures, sound effects, and in-game music, reflecting comprehensive multimedia integration.

\subsection{Mini Dialogue System}

The Outcomes-Based Edugame Design framework specifies narrative contextualization as essential to meaningful gameplay. Effective narrative requires answering fundamental questions: Why is the player engaging this experience? Who does the player inhabit? What are the player's objectives within a relatable context? Balancing narrative immersion against player autonomy presented design tension: excessive exposition overwhelms players and undermines agency; insufficient context obscures purpose and reduces engagement.

This design challenge yielded the Mini Dialogue System---a lightweight, unobtrusive narrative delivery mechanism. The nomenclature reflects both implementational parsimony (minimal code complexity) and experiential design (minimal screen real estate consumption). The system presents context-sensitive prompts accessible via screen-top interaction points. Prompts are triggered upon level initiation or upon entering specific spatial regions, delivering explicit gameplay instructions, critical information, or narrative dialogue without disrupting primary gameplay flow.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{DialogueSample.png}
\caption{Sample Dialogue}
\label{fig:dialogue_sample}
\end{figure}

Internationalization support was initially conceptualized to include Tagalog language options, anticipated to provide improved accessibility for target demographic members more fluent in the native Philippine language. Implementation was deferred due to development timeline constraints. Additionally, extensive terminology from the \chg{learning outcomes} (``sublimation,'' ``deposition,'' ``bonding'') and platform-specific action vocabulary (``tap,'' ``swipe,'' ``toggle'') resisted direct translation without semantic loss, potentially impairing comprehension for Tagalog-preferring learners. These linguistic and practical constraints motivated deferral of localization to post-release development phases.
