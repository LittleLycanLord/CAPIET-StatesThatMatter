\section{Literature Review on the Educational Challenges in the Philippines in both a General and Scientific Scope}

\input{chapters/chapter2/tables/table_educational_challenges}

The K–12 program, which is the current educational framework in the Philippines, has faced multiple challenges, particularly curriculum congestion \parencite{Kilag2024a}. The large volume of topics and activities required within limited instructional time places significant pressure on both teachers and students. Educators often struggle to cover all mandated competencies effectively, while students experience increased academic load and stress. Additional challenges include limited instructional resources and shortages of adequately trained teachers.

In response to these concerns, the Department of Education introduced the MATATAG Curriculum, which aims to address curriculum congestion and better respond to learners' needs. The MATATAG Curriculum emphasizes critical thinking, digital literacy, and global competence. However, as the curriculum is still in its early stages of implementation, continuous evaluation is necessary to assess its effectiveness and identify areas for improvement.

Despite the easing of pandemic restrictions, many schools continue to employ hybrid learning approaches. Research indicates that students' experiences with online and blended learning vary depending on factors such as access to resources, quality of teacher interaction, and socioeconomic conditions \parencite{Barrot2021}. Studies have shown that challenges in online learning environments are more commonly associated with home learning conditions rather than technological literacy, highlighting the need for accessible and flexible instructional tools.

In science education, curriculum reform has been driven by issues such as mismatched prerequisite knowledge and consistently low performance in national and international assessments. Under the MATATAG framework, science education aims to strengthen students' technological and scientific literacy to meet the demands of the 21st century and emerging industries \parencite{Diquito2024}. Studies on chemistry instruction indicate that student performance and engagement improve when instructional approaches incorporate interactive strategies such as computer simulations, inquiry-based learning, cooperative learning, and project-based e-learning \parencite{Vallespin2024,Vega2024}.

\section{Literature Review on Educational Serious Games}

The table below showcases the papers that cover educational serious games. It discusses the impact of serious games and use of outcome-based methodology for game design.

\input{chapters/chapter2/tables/table_serious_games}

Existing literature distinguishes serious games from gamification. Gamification refers to the integration of game design elements into non-game contexts, while serious games are complete digital games designed with both educational and entertainment goals. Research suggests that both approaches can positively influence learning outcomes, though they affect motivation differently. Gamification tends to enhance extrinsic motivation, whereas serious games are more strongly associated with intrinsic motivation \parencite{Ren2024}.

For serious games, outcome-based development methodologies are commonly recommended. In such approaches, learning objectives serve as the foundation for designing gameplay mechanics, narratives, and feedback systems \parencite{Sison2018}. This alignment ensures that educational goals remain central to the player experience and that gameplay directly supports learning outcomes.

Game-Based Learning (GBL) promotes both hands-on and minds-on learning, leading to deeper conceptual understanding \parencite{Byusa2022}. Research has shown that digital educational games can improve critical thinking skills, learning retention, and student motivation by presenting content in an engaging and interactive manner \parencite{Putri2022}.

Many existing chemistry educational games are puzzle-based, such as Sokobond Express and Element Solitaire (Elementeo), while others adopt role-playing or simulation-based formats \parencite{Hu2022}.

\input{chapters/chapter2/tables/table_stoichiometry}

Studies also highlight that difficult chemistry topics, such as stoichiometry, often benefit from game-based approaches due to their ability to increase motivation and reduce cognitive barriers. Even non-digital educational games, such as board games modeled after snakes and ladders, have demonstrated positive effects on students' motivation and learning outcomes in chemistry-related topics \parencite{Janah2024}.

\section{Literature Review on Bubble Shooter Games for Learning}

The table below showcases the research papers on bubble-shooter type games. The papers under this category explore how effective a bubble-shooter mechanic is for learning.

\input{chapters/chapter2/tables/table_bubble_shooter}

Bubble shooter games have been found to support memorization and visualization due to their repetitive and pattern-based mechanics \parencite{Maung2015}. Research-oriented bubble shooter games such as Bias Blaster and Chemistry Pop, though not commercially published, demonstrate the potential of this genre as an educational tool. These games show that bubble shooter mechanics can effectively support learning by reinforcing repetition, visual association, and strategic thinking.
