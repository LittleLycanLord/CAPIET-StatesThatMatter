\chapter{INTRODUCTION}

\section{Background of the Study}

A strong foundation in chemistry is essential for students early in their academic careers, as it enables them to understand more complex concepts and recognize their applications in real-world contexts. However, many students struggle with chemistry due to the abstract nature of its foundational topics and the limited opportunities to apply these concepts in practical settings.

One major factor contributing to student disengagement and cognitive overload is the structure of traditional learning materials, which often emphasize immediate mastery rather than gradual understanding. In many classroom environments, students are expected to apply large amounts of newly introduced information without sufficient time to practice or explore concepts meaningfully \parencite{Saputri2025}. As a result, chemistry is frequently perceived as difficult and unapproachable, leading to reduced motivation and interest among learners.

A hands-on and interactive approach to learning can help students better internalize abstract concepts by transforming them into tangible experiences \parencite{Bridges2015}. Interactive learning environments encourage active participation, allowing students to develop critical thinking and analytical skills through experimentation and problem-solving. Additionally, immediate feedback supports the correction of misconceptions, enabling students to reflect on and improve their understanding in real time \parencite{Blyznyuk2024}.

These factors significantly influence students' motivation and ability to engage with foundational chemistry topics, particularly those involving changes in matter. Before students can fully grasp phase changes, they must first master prerequisite concepts. However, rigid curriculum pacing often prevents learners from fully absorbing new ideas before progressing to subsequent topics, resulting in frustration and disengagement \parencite{Treagust2018}.

Given these challenges, there is a clear need for learning approaches that promote active engagement without overwhelming students. Instructional tools that balance accessibility, relevance, and interactivity can enhance comprehension while supporting the development of cognitive and critical thinking skills \parencite{Ren2024}. This study aims to develop a curriculum-aligned approach that serves as a supplementary learning tool, helping students transition from disinterest to engagement while fostering a more positive emotional experience with chemistry learning.

Furthermore, integrating a Game-Based Learning (GBL) approach as a supplement to traditional instruction may strengthen students' conceptual understanding and analytical abilities \parencite{Putri2022}. By embedding chemistry concepts within interactive gameplay, the study seeks to support long-term learning and improve students' confidence in engaging with foundational chemistry topics.

\section{Statement of the Problem}

A key challenge in Grade 6 chemistry education is bridging the gap between learning scientific concepts and applying them to real-world situations. While students are taught about changes in the state of matter \parencite{DepEd2024}, these concepts are often perceived as abstract and disconnected from everyday experiences. As a result, learners may struggle to recognize the relevance and practical value of chemistry knowledge outside the classroom. This disconnect has been associated with poor knowledge retention and difficulty applying scientific concepts in authentic contexts among Filipino students \parencite{Barrot2021}.

Traditional instructional methods tend to prioritize textbook-based learning and factual recall, often overlooking the importance of contextual understanding and experiential learning. \section{Scope and Limitations}

This study focuses on the development of [GAME NAME HERE] as a curriculum-aligned educational game designed to support the Grade 6 MATATAG First-Quarter Science syllabus \parencite{DepEd2024}. The game specifically targets learning outcomes related to changes in the state of matter, physical and chemical changes, and mixtures and separation techniques. Its bubble-shooter mechanics are intended to help students visualize chemical processes through interactive elements while encouraging strategic thinking. To prevent cognitive overload, the game limits its content to topics included in the official curriculum.

The game bridges classroom instruction and real-world application by embedding chemistry concepts into familiar scenarios, such as food preparation and household activities. Actions such as slicing, crushing, heating, and cooling are used to illustrate physical and chemical changes in a simplified manner appropriate to a Grade 6 learner's cognitive level. To maintain accessibility, the game avoids introducing excessive variables that may overwhelm students.

The study evaluates the game's effectiveness through pretests and posttests targeting specific competencies, as well as engagement metrics collected during gameplay. Assessment tasks are aligned with MATATAG performance standards, such as designing fair tests to compare physical changes. However, the study is limited by its duration and does not assess the long-term transfer of learning to future chemistry topics beyond the scope of the game.
