\chapter{RESEARCH METHODOLOGY}

This chapter describes the systematic approach employed to design, develop, and evaluate \textit{Wiz's Journey: Mastering the States of Matter}. The methodology integrates the Outcomes-Based Edugame Design framework \cite{Sison2018} with structured evaluation practices aligned with the conceptual framework (Section 2.5).

\section{Research Activities}

The development of the educational game was grounded in explicit research activities that informed the design direction and ensured pedagogical alignment throughout the development process.

\subsection{Research of Learning Outcomes and Pedagogical Approaches}

Prior to commencing game design, a comprehensive research phase was undertaken to establish the pedagogical foundation for \textit{Wiz's Journey}. This research comprised three complementary components:

\subsubsection{Curriculum Analysis and Learning Competency Clarification}

The MATATAG Grade 6 Science curriculum documents were reviewed to identify and clarify the specific learning competencies related to phase changes and states of matter. This analysis determined the precise learning outcomes (LOs) that would anchor the game design. The target competency---understanding phase transitions (melting, freezing, evaporation, condensation) and the relationship between heat transfer and state changes---was operationalized into explicit learning outcome mechanics (LOMs) that would structure both lower-order and higher-order gameplay challenges \cite{Sison2018}.

\subsubsection{Pedagogical Methodology Review}

Existing pedagogical approaches to teaching phase changes were examined, encompassing traditional lecture-based instruction, hands-on laboratory experimentation, visual demonstrations, and conceptual change strategies. This review informed the selection of constructivist and experiential learning principles (Section 2.4) as the theoretical foundations guiding interactive gameplay design. The review identified that effective instruction on phase changes requires students to move beyond memorization toward active conceptualization of particulate-level mechanisms underlying macroscopic observations (Samarapungavan et al., 2023).

\subsubsection{Review of Existing Educational Resources and Games}

Existing educational games, simulations, and learning aids designed to support phase change instruction were reviewed to identify design patterns, pedagogical strengths, and limitations. This comparative analysis ensured that \textit{Wiz's Journey} would employ evidence-based design practices while offering novel approaches to addressing documented learning challenges in this domain.

\section{Game Design Process}

The design and development of \textit{Wiz's Journey} followed the Outcomes-Based Edugame Design methodology proposed by Sison et al. (2018) \cite{Sison2018}, which operationalizes the alignment between learning outcomes, game mechanics, and iterative user feedback. The design process proceeded through three major stages, with development occurring from late November 2025 through March 2026.

\subsection{Stage 1: Learning Outcome Mechanics Planning and Prototyping (Late November -- Mid-December 2025)}

During this stage, the explicit learning outcomes derived from the curriculum analysis (Section 3.1.1) were translated into learning outcome mechanics---specific gameplay elements designed to support the achievement of each learning outcome \cite{Sison2018}. This stage involved:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Definition of learning outcome mechanics aligned with the six primary phase changes (melting, freezing, evaporation, condensation, sublimation, deposition)
    \item Prototyping of interactive mechanics allowing players to manipulate variables affecting phase transitions (temperature, particle count, bond stiffness, bond length)
    \item Validation that each mechanic directly instantiates the underlying scientific principle it is designed to teach
    \item Initial playtesting of prototype mechanics to assess feasibility and pedagogical potential
\end{itemize}

This stage ensured that the foundational game mechanics were theoretically grounded before proceeding to genre and narrative development.

\subsection{Stage 2: Genre and Premise Development (December 2025 -- January 2026)}

Following validation of learning outcome mechanics, the game genre and narrative premise were established to contextualize the mechanics within engaging gameplay. Decisions during this stage included:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Selection of the 2D platformer genre as appropriate for integrating phase change mechanics with navigation challenges
    \item Development of the game premise: players assume the role of ``Wiz,'' a young wizard apprentice solving environmental puzzles involving phase transitions
    \item Narrative framing of each level's challenges as requiring players to manipulate phase states to progress
    \item Integration of the narrative context with learning outcome mechanics to ensure mechanics served both gameplay and pedagogical objectives
\end{itemize}

This stage balanced entertainment value with pedagogical rigor, ensuring that gameplay progression was intrinsically motivated by learning objective achievement.

\subsection{Stage 3: Iterative Building and System Integration (Late January -- March 2026)}

The final stage involved incremental construction of the complete game learning environment (GLE) incorporating all designed systems, mechanics, and narrative elements. Following the Sison et al. (2018) framework \cite{Sison2018}, this stage proceeded through multiple iterative cycles, each consisting of:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Design LO Mechanic Implementation} -- Addition or refinement of gameplay mechanics supporting specific learning outcomes
    \item \textbf{Design Related Elements} -- Development of supporting game systems (particle lattice visualization, level progression, UI, narrative scaffolding)
    \item \textbf{Integrate with Previous Systems} -- Connection of newly developed mechanics with previously completed systems to ensure coherent gameplay
    \item \textbf{Playtesting and Evaluation} -- Structured testing with participants to identify usability issues, gameplay friction, and pedagogical alignment
    \item \textbf{Iteration and Refinement} -- Modification based on accumulated feedback before advancing to the next learning outcome mechanic
\end{enumerate}

This iterative approach, as conceptualized in the Conceptual Framework (Section 2.5), embedded continuous user testing throughout development rather than conducting comprehensive evaluation only upon completion. The embedded playtesting cycles systematically validated alignment between pedagogical theory and actual player experience, resolving implementation gaps incrementally.

By March 2026, the iterative building process yielded a complete, tested game build incorporating all designed learning outcome mechanics and supporting systems, positioning the game for formal evaluation.

\subsection{Recruitment and Evaluation Challenges}

The iterative development and evaluation phases encountered significant recruitment challenges that shaped the scope and implementation of the study. Initial contact with Inchican Elementary School was made in December 2025 but received no response during that period. Subsequent attempts to recruit Grade 6 students from Seven Pillars Catholic School and Princeton Academy were made from mid to late February 2026, but neither school responded to communication attempts. Inchican Elementary School was finally confirmed as a partner in early March 2026, providing access to a limited number of Grade 6 students willing to participate. These recruitment constraints, attributed primarily to the graduation season when students in the target grade were transitioning to secondary education and unavailable for research participation, necessitated adaptation of the planned evaluation methodology.

To address anticipated recruitment challenges, community outreach strategies were implemented preemptively during the formative evaluation phase, including public dissemination of the game through the Itch.io distribution platform and Facebook. While these platforms provided broader access to the game for feedback purposes, they did not substantially increase the number of consenting Grade 6 participants available for structured, in-person evaluation sessions.

Despite these constraints, the data gathered during the limited formative playtesting (three participants) proved valuable in amplifying signals for actual adjustments that informed the summative evaluation design. The feedback from these three formative participants guided substantive refinements to gameplay mechanics, UI clarity, and overall user experience before the summative evaluation, ensuring that the final build represented an iteratively improved product. This adaptation demonstrated the critical value of even small-scale formative testing in validating pedagogical alignment and usability before formal evaluation.

\subsection{Game-Based Learning Environment Evaluation}

The evaluation of \textit{Wiz's Journey} employed a mixed-methods approach combining quantitative usability and engagement measurement with qualitative player feedback. Evaluation occurred in two phases corresponding to the Process Component structure described in Section 2.5.2.

\subsubsection{Formative Evaluation Phase (Iterative Development)}

During the iterative building stage (Stage 3 above), formative evaluation data were collected to guide ongoing refinement:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sample}: Three (3) Grade 6 student participants recruited through convenience sampling and institutional partnerships with Inchican Elementary School. Community outreach was conducted concurrently through Facebook and the Itch.io game distribution platform to gather feedback from a broader audience and extend reach to additional schools, though these channels did not yield sufficient consenting Grade 6 participants for in-person evaluation
    \item \textbf{Procedure}: Participants engaged in structured play sessions with development builds, followed by semi-structured interviews and MEEGA+KIDS-based survey feedback \cite{vonWangenheim2020}
    \item \textbf{Data Collection}: Responses were gathered via Google Forms surveys and face-to-face interviews conducted after gameplay
    \item \textbf{Analysis}: Feedback was qualitatively analyzed to identify usability barriers, confusion points, and suggestions for game refinement
    \item \textbf{Iteration}: Identified issues were prioritized and addressed in subsequent development cycles before the next formative test session
\end{itemize}

This formative phase directly informed the iterative refinement process, ensuring that the finalized build incorporated player-informed improvements.

\subsubsection{Summative Evaluation Phase (Final Structured Evaluation)}

Upon completion of iterative development, a formal evaluation of the finalized game build was conducted:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sample}: Eighteen (18) Grade 6 student participants recruited through institutional partnership with Inchican Elementary School
    
    \item \textbf{Procedure and Data Collection}: Participants completed a structured evaluation session consisting of three sequential components: (1) completion of a 25-question pretest (approximately 15 minutes) assessing baseline knowledge of phase changes, (2) gameplay with the finalized build (approximately 30 minutes), and (3) completion of a 25-question posttest (approximately 15 minutes) assessing post-intervention knowledge. The posttest employed different questions from the pretest but maintained equivalent content coverage and difficulty level. Due to time constraints within the school setting, participants were unable to complete the MEEGA+KIDS survey instrument following gameplay. However, extensive qualitative evidence was gathered through systematic visual observation of participant reactions, frustrations, body language, and gameplay behaviors during the evaluation session
    
    \item \textbf{Analysis}: Quantitative analysis of learning gains employed statistical hypothesis testing. Shapiro-Wilk tests were conducted first to determine normality of the pretest-posttest difference scores. If the data were normally distributed, parametric paired t-tests were performed to assess statistically significant differences between pre- and post-intervention knowledge. If data violated normality assumptions (non-parametric distribution), Wilcoxon Signed-Rank tests were conducted instead. These tests provided statistical evidence regarding whether observed improvements between pretest and posttest scores were statistically significant, allowing for inference beyond the sample to the broader population of Grade 6 students. Qualitative observational data were analyzed thematically to identify patterns in participant engagement, frustration points, and behavioral indicators of learning or confusion
\end{itemize}

The summative phase provided empirical evidence regarding the game's achievement of the stated objectives related to usability, engagement, and learning outcomes.

\subsection{Consent for Participation}

Given that all participants were Grade 6 students (minors), rigorous informed consent procedures were implemented to ensure ethical compliance and participant protection:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Parental Consent and Student Assent}: A written informed consent form was developed and reviewed collaboratively with concurrent research teams engaging the same participant population. The form clearly described the study's purpose, procedures, data collection methods, confidentiality safeguards, and participants' rights. Separate assent forms were provided to Grade 6 student participants, allowing them to provide informed agreement to participate independently of parental consent. Both the parental consent forms and student assent forms were distributed through children's teachers, requiring written signatures from both guardians and students prior to any participation
    
    \item \textbf{Data Confidentiality and Participant Rights}: All forms, surveys, and study materials explicitly stated that participants could withdraw from evaluation activities at any time without penalty, that their data would remain confidential, and that data could be deleted upon request. No personally identifiable information was included in subsequent data analysis or reporting
    
    \item \textbf{Safeguarding Minors}: The requirement for both written parental consent and student assent served as primary safeguards for minor participants, respecting both parental authority and the child's own voice and autonomy in research participation. Additionally, all evaluation sessions were conducted in school settings with supervision by school personnel, and no data collection occurred without documented parental and student authorization
\end{itemize}

These procedures ensured that participation was informed, voluntary, and protective of participant rights and privacy.

\section{Research Instruments and Data Collection}

The study employed structured instruments aligned with the evaluation components outlined in the Conceptual Framework (Section 2.5).

\subsection{MEEGA+KIDS Usability and Engagement Instrument}

The MEEGA+KIDS instrument was designed to assess multiple dimensions of player experience \cite{vonWangenheim2020}:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Focused Attention}: Concentration during gameplay and ability to maintain engagement
    \item \textbf{Perceived Learning}: Participants' perception of learning value and knowledge gain
    \item \textbf{Satisfaction}: Overall enjoyment and contentment with the gaming experience
    \item \textbf{Usability}: Ease of understanding game mechanics and navigating game systems
    \item \textbf{Challenge}: Appropriateness of difficulty and balance between accessibility and cognitive demand
    \item \textbf{Intrinsic Motivation}: Engagement driven by enjoyment rather than external rewards
\end{itemize}

Participants responded to Likert-scale statements (typically five-point scales: Strongly Disagree to Strongly Agree) assessing each dimension. This instrument provided quantifiable measures of engagement and usability suitable for characterizing player experience.

For the purposes of this study, these six dimensions were conceptually consolidated into five primary categories to facilitate focused analysis of player experience:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Usability and Interface Design}: Encompasses the ``Usability'' dimension, assessing whether participants could effectively navigate game menus, understand on-screen prompts, and operate game mechanics without excessive confusion or frustration
    
    \item \textbf{Engagement and Experience}: Synthesizes ``Focused Attention'' and ``Intrinsic Motivation'' dimensions, measuring whether participants remained mentally engaged with the game and whether motivation was driven by inherent enjoyment rather than external pressures
    
    \item \textbf{Perceived Learning Support}: Directly corresponds to ``Perceived Learning'' dimension, capturing participants' subjective assessment of whether gameplay supported their understanding of phase change concepts
    
    \item \textbf{Aesthetics and Challenge}: Combines ``Satisfaction'' and ``Challenge'' dimensions, evaluating both the visual and interactive appeal of the game and the appropriateness of difficulty progression relative to player skill levels
    
    \item \textbf{Open-Ended Feedback}: Captures participant suggestions, qualitative observations about strengths and weaknesses, and thematic patterns emerging from participant responses that the Likert-scale instrument may not capture
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Qualitative Feedback Collection}

In addition to the MEEGA+KIDS instrument, participants provided open-ended qualitative feedback through:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Semi-Structured Interviews}: Face-to-face conversations during and immediately following gameplay sessions, with participants answering guiding questions about strengths, challenges, confusion points, and suggestions
    \item \textbf{Open-Ended Questionnaires}: Written responses to prompts addressing perceived learning value, difficulty appropriateness, enjoyment factors, and recommendations for improvement
\end{itemize}

Qualitative data provided rich contextual understanding of participant experience beyond numerical measurements, enabling identification of specific usability barriers and design opportunities.

\subsection{Visual Observation of Participant Behavior}

Beyond structured survey instruments and interviews, systematic visual observation of participant behavior during gameplay sessions provided rich qualitative evidence regarding engagement, frustration, and learning processes. Throughout evaluation sessions, researcher observations documented:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Engagement Indicators}: Participant body language, facial expressions, and posture during gameplay (e.g., leaning forward during challenging moments, relaxed posture during familiar mechanics)
    \item \textbf{Frustration and Confusion Points}: Observable reactions to difficulty spikes, unclear UI elements, or unintuitive mechanics, such as hesitation, repeated failed attempts, or verbal expressions of confusion
    \item \textbf{Interaction Patterns}: How participants approached level challenges, whether they experimented with mechanics, sought hints, or made strategic decisions
    \item \textbf{Learning Indicators}: Observable changes in approach between early and later gameplay, suggesting internalization of game concepts or learning from failure
    \item \textbf{Social Behavior}: Interactions with researchers or peers during gameplay, including questions asked, comments made, and collaborative behaviors
\end{itemize}

These observational data were documented through researcher field notes recorded during and immediately following evaluation sessions, providing contextual richness and non-verbal evidence of participant experience that complemented quantitative and structured qualitative measures.

\subsection{Researcher Field Notes and Observational Documentation}

Throughout the iterative building phase (Stage 3), the researcher maintained detailed field notes documenting observations during playtesting sessions:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Bug occurrence and severity observations (e.g., crashes, progression blocks, visual glitches)
    \item Gameplay progression issues and bottlenecks observed during participant sessions
    \item Player confusion points and mechanic clarity concerns noted from participant reactions
    \item UI navigation difficulties observed or expressed by participants
    \item Performance stability observations (e.g., frame rate drops, loading delays)
    \item Reflective session notes and developer observations regarding the alignment between intended and actual gameplay experiences
\end{itemize}

These field notes provided qualitative documentation of identified issues and observed resolutions across development iterations, enabling assessment of improvement trends and informing design refinements.

\section{Data Analysis Methods}

Analysis methods were selected to align with the research questions and evaluation objectives.

\subsection{Quantitative Analysis of Learning Outcomes}

Pretest-posttest score differences were analyzed using formal statistical hypothesis testing. First, a Shapiro-Wilk normality test was conducted to determine whether the distribution of pretest-posttest difference scores conformed to a normal distribution. Based on the results of this normality assessment:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{If normally distributed}: A paired-samples t-test was performed to assess whether mean posttest scores differed significantly from mean pretest scores, providing evidence of statistically significant learning gains
    \item \textbf{If non-parametric (non-normal distribution)}: A Wilcoxon Signed-Rank test was used as the non-parametric alternative to the t-test, assessing whether posttest scores were statistically significantly higher than pretest scores
\end{itemize}

These statistical tests allowed inference regarding whether observed improvements between pretest and posttest represented genuine learning differences beyond what would be expected by random variation. All quantitative analysis was conducted using appropriate statistical software with alpha level set at 0.05 for determination of statistical significance.

\subsection{Qualitative Analysis of Observations and Feedback}

Qualitative data from field notes, interviews, and open-ended questionnaire responses were analyzed using thematic coding:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Initial Coding}: Observational and interview data were reviewed inductively to identify emergent themes and patterns without predetermined categories
    \item \textbf{Theme Development}: Codes were organized into higher-order thematic categories representing distinct aspects of participant experience and identified usability concerns (e.g., ``Mechanic Clarity,'' ``Engagement Factors,'' ``Difficulty Appropriateness,'' ``Learning Perception,'' ``Frustration Points'')
    \item \textbf{Pattern Identification}: Frequency of theme occurrence was documented to distinguish high-consensus observations from isolated concerns
    \item \textbf{Integration with Quantitative Data}: Qualitative themes were integrated with quantitative findings to provide comprehensive characterization of player learning and experience
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Iterative Development Improvement Analysis}

Field notes from the iterative building phase (Stage 3) and formative evaluation were analyzed descriptively to assess systematic improvements across development cycles:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Reduction in bug frequency and severity of reported issues across iteration cycles
    \item Improvement in gameplay stability observations (reduction in crashes, progression blocks)
    \item Resolution of recurring usability concerns noted in player interactions
    \item Comparison of early vs. late iteration observations regarding mechanic clarity and engagement
\end{itemize}

These analyses provided evidence of systematic improvement driven by iterative user feedback and researcher observations, validating the effectiveness of the embedded evaluation and refinement approach.

\section{Ethical Considerations}

This study adhered to established ethical principles for research involving human participants, particularly minors:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Informed Consent}: Written parental consent was obtained prior to any participant involvement, with clear disclosure of study procedures and participant rights
    \item \textbf{Voluntary Participation}: Participants and parents were informed of the voluntary nature of participation and the right to withdraw without penalty
    \item \textbf{Confidentiality and Privacy}: No personally identifiable information was retained in data analysis or reporting. Participant responses were treated as confidential, and individual data could be deleted upon request
    \item \textbf{Protection of Minors}: The requirement for parental written consent, combined with evaluation in supervised school settings, provided safeguards appropriate to the minor status of participants
    \item \textbf{Appropriate Data Use}: All collected data were used exclusively for academic research purposes related to this thesis study
\end{itemize}
