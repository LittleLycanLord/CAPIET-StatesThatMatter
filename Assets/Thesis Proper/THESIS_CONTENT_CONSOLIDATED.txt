=== ABSTRACT (ENGLISH) ===
==========================

Chemistry education contains foundational concepts that are often perceived as difficult for students to understand, particularly in terms of their application to real-world situations. This difficulty may lead to cognitive overload, especially when instruction lacks opportunities for practical engagement. Interactive learning approaches allow students to actively apply and develop their analytical skills while discovering how chemistry concepts relate to everyday experiences.

This study explores the use of Game-Based Learning (GBL) through the development of [GAME NAME HERE], an educational serious game designed as a learning material for Grade 6 students under the MATATAG curriculum in the Philippines. The game focuses on physical and chemical changes of matter, expanding students’ understanding beyond the three common states of matter. Through interactive gameplay, students engage with concepts such as phase changes and heat transfer while learning to distinguish physical changes from chemical changes.

The primary goal of this study is to improve students’ conceptual understanding, motivation, and emotional engagement by reinforcing hands-on learning and real-world applications of chemistry concepts. This project aims to provide both students and teachers with an accessible and engaging instructional resource that supports visualization, experimentation, and the development of analytical skills in basic chemistry education.

Keywords: physical and chemical changes, game-based learning, educational serious game

--- Section: Background of the Study ---

A strong foundation in chemistry is essential for students early in their academic careers, as it enables them to understand more complex concepts and recognize their applications in real-world contexts. However, many students struggle with chemistry due to the abstract nature of its foundational topics and the limited opportunities to apply these concepts in practical settings.

One major factor contributing to student disengagement and cognitive overload is the structure of traditional learning materials, which often emphasize immediate mastery rather than gradual understanding. In many classroom environments, students are expected to apply large amounts of newly introduced information without sufficient time to practice or explore concepts meaningfully. As a result, chemistry is frequently perceived as difficult and unapproachable, leading to reduced motivation and interest among learners.

A hands-on and interactive approach to learning can help students better internalize abstract concepts by transforming them into tangible experiences. Interactive learning environments encourage active participation, allowing students to develop critical thinking and analytical skills through experimentation and problem-solving. Additionally, immediate feedback supports the correction of misconceptions, enabling students to reflect on and improve their understanding in real time.

These factors significantly influence students’ motivation and ability to engage with foundational chemistry topics, particularly those involving changes in matter. Before students can fully grasp phase changes, they must first master prerequisite concepts. However, rigid curriculum pacing often prevents learners from fully absorbing new ideas before progressing to subsequent topics, resulting in frustration and disengagement.

Given these challenges, there is a clear need for learning approaches that promote active engagement without overwhelming students. Instructional tools that balance accessibility, relevance, and interactivity can enhance comprehension while supporting the development of cognitive and critical thinking skills. This study aims to develop a curriculum-aligned approach that serves as a supplementary learning tool, helping students transition from disinterest to engagement while fostering a more positive emotional experience with chemistry learning.

Furthermore, integrating a Game-Based Learning (GBL) approach as a supplement to traditional instruction may strengthen students’ conceptual understanding and analytical abilities. By embedding chemistry concepts within interactive gameplay, the study seeks to support long-term learning and improve students’ confidence in engaging with foundational chemistry topics.

Consequently, students may understand what happens during a physical or chemical change but fail to grasp why it occurs or how it applies to real-life scenarios.

--- Section: Literature Review on the Educational Challenges in the Philippines in both a General and Scientific Scope ---

The K–12 program, which is the current educational framework in the Philippines, has faced multiple challenges, particularly curriculum congestion. The large volume of topics and activities required within limited instructional time places significant pressure on both teachers and students. Educators often struggle to cover all mandated competencies effectively, while students experience increased academic load and stress. Additional challenges include limited instructional resources and shortages of adequately trained teachers.

In response to these concerns, the Department of Education introduced the MATATAG Curriculum, which aims to address curriculum congestion and better respond to learners’ needs. The MATATAG Curriculum emphasizes critical thinking, digital literacy, and global competence. However, as the curriculum is still in its early stages of implementation, continuous evaluation is necessary to assess its effectiveness and identify areas for improvement.

Despite the easing of pandemic restrictions, many schools continue to employ hybrid learning approaches. Research indicates that students’ experiences with online and blended learning vary depending on factors such as access to resources, quality of teacher interaction, and socioeconomic conditions. Studies have shown that challenges in online learning environments are more commonly associated with home learning conditions rather than technological literacy, highlighting the need for accessible and flexible instructional tools.

In science education, curriculum reform has been driven by issues such as mismatched prerequisite knowledge and consistently low performance in national and international assessments. Under the MATATAG framework, science education aims to strengthen students’ technological and scientific literacy to meet the demands of the 21st century and emerging industries. Studies on chemistry instruction indicate that student performance and engagement improve when instructional approaches incorporate interactive strategies such as computer simulations, inquiry-based learning, cooperative learning, and project-based e-learning.

--- Section: Literature Review on Educational Serious Games ---

Existing literature distinguishes serious games from gamification. Gamification refers to the integration of game design elements into non-game contexts, while serious games are complete digital games designed with both educational and entertainment goals. Research suggests that both approaches can positively influence learning outcomes, though they affect motivation differently. Gamification tends to enhance extrinsic motivation, whereas serious games are more strongly associated with intrinsic motivation.

For serious games, outcome-based development methodologies are commonly recommended. In such approaches, learning objectives serve as the foundation for designing gameplay mechanics, narratives, and feedback systems. This alignment ensures that educational goals remain central to the player experience and that gameplay directly supports learning outcomes.

--- Section: Literature Review on Chemistry Game-Based Learning and Existing Chemistry Games ---

Game-Based Learning (GBL) promotes both hands-on and minds-on learning, leading to deeper conceptual understanding. Research has shown that digital educational games can improve critical thinking skills, learning retention, and student motivation by presenting content in an engaging and interactive manner.

Many existing chemistry educational games are puzzle-based, such as Sokobond Express and Element Solitaire (Elementeo), while others adopt role-playing or simulation-based formats. Studies also highlight that difficult chemistry topics, such as stoichiometry, often benefit from game-based approaches due to their ability to increase motivation and reduce cognitive barriers. Even non-digital educational games, such as board games modeled after snakes and ladders, have demonstrated positive effects on students’ motivation and learning outcomes in chemistry-related topics.

--- Section: Literature Review on Bubble Shooter Games for Learning ---

Bubble shooter games have been found to support memorization and visualization due to their repetitive and pattern-based mechanics. Research-oriented bubble shooter games such as Bias Blaster and Chemistry Pop, though not commercially published, demonstrate the potential of this genre as an educational tool. These games show that bubble shooter mechanics can effectively support learning by reinforcing repetition, visual association, and strategic thinking.

--- Section: Game-Concept ---

[GAME NAME HERE] is a 2D stylized educational game set in a brightly colored neighborhood and follows a chemist as she assists the community with daily tasks. The challenges focus on core chemistry topics such as states of matter and, more importantly, distinguishing between physical and chemical changes in everyday life. The game combines educational gaming with puzzle and simulation elements. The bubble-shooter mechanics place it within the puzzle genre, emphasizing logic, accuracy, and planning. --- Section: Formative User Testing Observations ---

Formative user testing was conducted with Grade 6 students from Inchican Elementary School using a playable prototype that included the Tutorial Level, Bubble Shooter Level 1, and the Sorting Mini Game. Testing was conducted face-to-face, allowing direct observation of player behavior, engagement, and comprehension. Students demonstrated an overall understanding of gameplay mechanics and responded positively to visual cues and immediate feedback, despite their current science curriculum focusing on topics outside states of matter.

Feedback indicated that some levels were perceived as too easy and quickly memorized, suggesting a need for increased challenge variety. Students also commented on improving visual consistency in the sorting minigame and adding more objects to extend engagement. Language accessibility emerged as a key consideration, with requests for Filipino or Tagalog text. Overall, students expressed enjoyment of the game experience and interest in playing a completed version. These observations will inform future refinements to difficulty balancing, visual design, and language support.

=== CHAPTER 5: METHODOLOGY ===
==============================

--- Section: Research Design ---

This study employs a mixed-methods research approach. Qualitative data will be collected through one-on-one interviews conducted during and after game development to identify student preferences and evaluate enjoyment. Quantitative data will be gathered through pretests and posttests administered over one month to assess learning gains related to changes in the state of matter.

--- Section: Data Collection ---

Primary data will be collected from interviews and pretest and posttest results involving 20 Grade 6 students in a face-to-face setting. Each researcher will interview five students, with interviews audio-recorded for transcription and analysis. Pretests will assess baseline knowledge through a 25-item quiz administered prior to gameplay. Posttests of equal length will be administered following gameplay sessions monitored weekly by the research team.

Secondary data will include in-game analytics designed to track engagement and progression. These data will be collected ethically, stored securely, and used to supplement findings from tests and interviews.

--- Section: Data Analysis ---

Repeated measures analysis of variance (ANOVA) will be used to compare pretest and posttest scores. Qualitative interview data will be analyzed using thematic analysis, involving transcription, coding, theme identification, and interpretation to synthesize findings.

--- Section: Ethical Considerations ---

Informed consent will be obtained from all participants and their parents or guardians. Participant anonymity will be maintained, and students may withdraw at any time without penalty. Potential risks, such as content misalignment with classroom instruction, will be mitigated through collaboration with teachers and iterative playtesting. All participant data will be securely disposed of upon completion of the study.
